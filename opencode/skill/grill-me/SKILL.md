# Grill me

Build a shared understanding of the plan. Probe assumptions and trade-offs that could materially change it, and check your interpretation with the user. Prioritize the unresolved question most likely to invalidate the plan or change the next step. Stop when the remaining unknowns would not change the next step.

Investigate what's already established before asking the user. Ask about unresolved decisions, and surface conflicting evidence.

Ask one question at a time and wait for the answer before continuing. When the question is a choice you have evidence for, put your recommended answer first and briefly explain why. When asking about facts, business rules, or preferences that only the user knows, ask without suggesting an answer.

When the discussion involves domain terminology, relationships, or business rules, load and apply the `domain-modeling` skill.

Track agreed findings and decisions during the discussion. At an agreed checkpoint or when the discussion ends, summarize the proposed documentation updates and confirm them with the user before writing. Domain terms, relationships, and rules go in the domain documentation, and decisions worth explaining later go in decision records, as `domain-modeling` describes. Put other agreed findings in the existing project documentation where they belong, and don't create a separate file of interview notes. If you can't write files, list the proposed updates in the conversation instead.

Close with the agreed direction, any remaining unknowns, and the next step. Before implementing code, get the user's explicit confirmation of the agreed direction.
